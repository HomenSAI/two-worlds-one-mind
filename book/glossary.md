# Glossary

[Contents](../README.md) · [Deutsch](de/glossary.md) · [← 8 · Field guide](08-field-guide.md) · Next: [Notes and sources →](notes.md)

---

**Bridge** — the overlap of the two worlds: mechanical systems (machines, steel, production) and
software (IT). Where the author brings the most: machine logic, digitalisation of production, AI in
real processes.

**Caesar cipher** — one of the oldest ciphers: every letter is shifted by a fixed number of places
in the alphabet. `LPDJLQDWLRQ` with a shift of −3 reads **IMAGINATION**.

**CNC** — computer numerical control: a machine tool driven by a program. Machines plus code — the
two worlds in one device.

**Commissioning** — launching equipment on the customer's site and staying until it runs as
specified.

**Delegation (as a director)** — task, deadline, resources, control. Delegate what you can define
and verify; step in only when it really matters.

**Division of work** — routine goes to machines, with checks; people keep what only people can do.

**From code to intent** — the shift described in this book: the human part of building moves from
writing code to stating clearly what is wanted and verifying the result.

**HomenS.A Inc.** — the author's name mark: *Homen*(ko) *S*(erhii) *A*(leksandrovych). The mark —
a gold gear (mechanical systems) and a teal circuit (IT) — stands for "Two worlds. One mind." A
brand name only — not a registered company or legal entity.

**Homo roboticus** — in this book, the working name for a speculative next step after
*Homo sapiens*: mind carried by machines, across worlds. Not a biological term. The phrase has
been used by other authors before; see [Notes and sources](notes.md#prior-use-of-the-term-homo-roboticus).

**Local AI** — running AI models on hardware you control. Whether data really stays local depends
on the complete configuration: tools and plug-ins, network access, telemetry, logs, synchronisation
and backups. See the [checklist in the Field guide](08-field-guide.md#6-know-where-your-data-goes).

**Mechanical systems** — in this book, the physical world of machines, mechanisms and production,
also called "steel" or physical engineering. Not computer hardware. (Earlier drafts called this
side "hardware (non-IT)"; the longer name avoids the usual meaning of the word.)

**Mind uploading** — the hypothetical transfer of a person's mind into a machine. Today science
fiction; an open question.

**Philosophical management** — the author's own name, not a standard step in programming, for the
level above high-level programming: managing work by intent, ideas and judgement rather than by
code, while still verifying the result.

**Physical AI** — AI that acts in the physical world through robots, drives, valves, sensors and
machines — and is tested there.

**Verification loop** — question → set-up → run → measure → verified? If not, back to set-up. Not
done until verified.
