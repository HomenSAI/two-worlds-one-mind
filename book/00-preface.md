I have spent my working life in two worlds at once.

The first is physical: machines, production lines, steel, the shop floor, a truck leaving with
equipment for a customer in another country. The second is digital: computers since school,
cryptography in the 1990s, 3D modelling, GPU rigs, and now artificial intelligence.

For a long time these two worlds talked to each other through people — through me, a designer,
a programmer. I wrote the logic of a machine by hand, the programmer turned it into code, the
workshop turned it into steel. Today something has changed. The distance between an idea and a
working thing has become shorter than ever. A person who can describe clearly what he wants —
and check what he gets — can now build things that used to need a department.

This book is my attempt to write down how I see that change and how I work with it. It is not
a textbook and not a forecast. It is a working position: ideas I have tested, principles I keep,
and a vision I believe in.

## How the book is built

- **Chapters 1–3** — the view: where it comes from, the manifesto, the principles.
- **Chapters 4–6** — the practice: physical AI, the lab, the path behind it.
- **Chapter 7** — the vision: where it is all going, in the longest possible view.
- **Chapter 8** — the field guide: the same ideas as checklists for your own work.

Each chapter links to the page of [homensai.com](https://homensai.com/) where the same idea is
shown as an animated diagram. The site is the short version; this book is the long one.

## A note on honesty

Everything here is my personal view. Numbers about my companies are rounded, from my own
records. Where an idea is not mine, I say whose it is. Where something is science fiction
today, I call it science fiction. You will find these notes in [Notes and sources](notes.md).

*Serhii Khomenko, Stuttgart region, October 2026*
