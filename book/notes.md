## About this text

- The book is assembled from the author's own dictated texts (September–October 2026) and the
  pages of [homensai.com](https://homensai.com/) (site version 2.11 for edition 1.0; see the
  [provenance table](../docs/provenance.md)). Writing, structuring and editing were done with AI
  assistance — under the author's direction and review, which is itself an example of the method
  described in [Chapter 3](03-principles.md). The revision for edition 1.1 followed a written
  audit; the traceability is in [docs/audit-resolution.md](../docs/audit-resolution.md).
- Everything is the author's personal view, unless stated otherwise. Statements about the author's
  own career come from his records and memory; they were not independently verified for this
  book.

## Prior use of the term Homo roboticus

The phrase *Homo roboticus* is not new. Examples of earlier use, found in a web check on
5 October 2026 (their content was not reviewed in depth, and they use the phrase for different
things):

- Hernández Ramírez, Greco (2017). *Homo roboticus: Ensayos sobre la creatividad científica en
  biología.* Siglo XXI Editores, Mexico. ISBN 978-607-03-0846-8. (Spanish; essays on scientific
  creativity in biology.)
- Falls, Susan (2018). "Homo Roboticus." *Anthropology News* 59(4): e179–e182.
  <https://doi.org/10.1111/AN.923>. (Robots, labour and citizenship.)
- Yuhico, Mayet Ligad (2019). *Homo Roboticus.* Self-published. ISBN 978-1-7334-5360-8. (A
  speculative novel.)
- Armstrong, Rob (2023). *Homo Roboticus: The Inner Human Robot Revealed By Sleepwalking and
  Hypnosis.* (E-book; publisher details differ between sellers and were not verified.)
- Other titles and papers also use it, for instance in work on robotics and society and in
  writing on human extinction and space; no complete survey was made.

The author does not claim to have coined the phrase. What this book puts forward as the author's
own is the **vision built around it**: life carried by machines through the Universe;
consciousness transfer as one possible way for an individual to travel there; "the less routine,
the more ideas"; the break-it-down approach to uncertainty. Related, earlier work by others
exists on each of these themes, and this book does not claim scientific priority for any of them.

## Quotation and sources

- **Albert Einstein**, "Imagination is more important than knowledge." Interview by George
  Sylvester Viereck, "What Life Means to Einstein", *The Saturday Evening Post*, 26 October 1929.
  The sentence appears in that interview. Used in [Chapter 2](02-manifesto.md) to support the
  point that imagination matters; it is a remark in an interview, not a research result.
- **Hans Moravec** (1988). *Mind Children: The Future of Robot and Human Intelligence.* Harvard
  University Press. ISBN 978-0-674-57616-2. Used in [Chapter 7](07-vision.md) as an earlier
  account of machines as successors of human minds. Moravec's argument is his own; this book does
  not restate it.
- **John von Neumann**, *Theory of Self-Reproducing Automata*, edited and completed by Arthur W.
  Burks (University of Illinois Press, 1966). The original work concerns abstract self-reproducing
  automata, not space probes. The application to interstellar probes came later, for example
  Robert A. Freitas Jr. (1980), "A Self-Reproducing Interstellar Probe", *Journal of the British
  Interplanetary Society* 33: 251–264.

## Science facts used

Checked on 5 October 2026. The links are to sources that may be updated.

- One-way radio delay Earth–Mars: 3 to 22 minutes, depending on the positions of the planets, and
  why real-time control is not possible: NASA,
  [Speaking Percy's language](https://science.nasa.gov/blog/speaking-percys-language-how-to-talk-so-perseverance-will-listen/).
- Voyager 1: launched 5 September 1977; entered interstellar space in August 2012; still
  operating with a reduced set of instruments:
  [NASA, Voyager 1](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/voyager/voyager-1/). NASA gives
  18 November 2026 as the date on which Voyager 1 will be one light-day from Earth
  ([current status](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/voyager/where-are-voyager-1-and-voyager-2-now/)).
  The spacecraft's status changes over time; check the NASA pages for the current state.
- Milky Way: about 100,000 light-years across (rounded; estimates vary):
  [NASA](https://science.nasa.gov/universe/exoplanets/our-milky-way-galaxy-how-big-is-space/).
- Dates of human evolution in [Chapter 7](07-vision.md) are rounded and simplified, and the
  species do not form a single line. Overview of the fossil record:
  [Smithsonian National Museum of Natural History, Human Origins](https://humanorigins.si.edu/evidence/human-fossils/species).
  For *Homo sapiens* around 300,000 years ago: Hublin, J.-J. et al. (2017), "New fossils from Jebel
  Irhoud, Morocco and the pan-African origin of *Homo sapiens*", *Nature* 546: 289–292,
  <https://doi.org/10.1038/nature22336>.
- Estimates of how quickly self-replicating probes could spread through the Galaxy are
  speculative and depend on assumptions; the chapter says so.
